# ADR 0143: Material social pressure drives political legitimacy

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Country legitimacy previously reacted to aggregate growth, fiscal balance, random political variation, and sovereign default. Household unemployment duration, deprivation memory, debt distress, and public-service quality were economically material but politically disconnected. This made politics insensitive to who actually absorbed economic losses.

## Decision

At every annual closure each region derives a persistent, fingerprinted social-pressure observation from authoritative household and service evidence. Chronic unemployment uses only currently unemployed cohorts and up to 24 observed unemployment months. Livelihood stress combines persistent deprivation memory with up to 24 months of debt distress. Public-service shortfall measures the gap in healthcare, infrastructure, and administrative delivery.

The regional combined score weights livelihood stress three parts, chronic unemployment two parts, and service shortfall one part. Country pressure is weighted by regional population rather than treating regions as equally large. A neutral pressure level of 3,000 basis points has no legitimacy effect; lower pressure supports legitimacy by at most 150 basis points per year, while maximum pressure reduces it by 350. Growth, fiscal balance, deterministic random variation, and debt restructuring retain their existing independent effects.

Typed events record every regional component and the population-weighted country effect. The yearly chronicle names the most pressured region, explains the components, and reports the resulting legitimacy movement. Severe pressure receives importance below acute rationing and institutional collapse but above routine activity.

## Consequences

Material outcomes now close a broad political feedback loop. Persistent unemployment, household distress, and weak services can erode legitimacy even when headline output is acceptable; broadly stable conditions can create bounded recovery. Large regions matter in proportion to their population, and the entire chain is replayable and inspectable.

The model still has one aggregate legitimacy value per country and no explicit constituencies, ideology, elections, protest organizations, or regional political representation. The next gate should retain regional interests over time and let policy choices create identifiable winners and losers before introducing parties or electoral mechanics.
