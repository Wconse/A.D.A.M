# ADR 0079: Liquidity-bounded sales-tax closure

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

ADR 0078 made annual regional output equal realized household trade, but country revenue was still generated by an aggregate output formula. No taxpayer lost cash when the state gained revenue, so the fiscal ledger could create public resources without a corresponding private payment.

The Stage 0 chain needs one executable tax channel before adding a full tax code. It must preserve money, remain deterministic, expose non-payment when firms lack liquidity, and feed the existing treasury, debt, and political closure.

## Decision

At each economic-year closure, every firm is assessed a 20 percent sales tax on the sum of its twelve retained monthly realized-sales observations.

The paid amount is bounded by both the liability and the firm's available cash:

```text
taxable sales = sum(realized monthly sales revenue)
liability = taxable sales × 20%
paid = min(liability, available firm cash)
```

The payment is debited from the firm before annual country accounts are applied. Country revenue is the checked sum of tax payments by firms located in its regions. Spending remains policy-derived from realized regional output; treasury and public debt close the resulting balance.

Each assessment emits `FirmSalesTaxPaid` with taxable sales, liability, and paid amount. This distinguishes statutory liability from realized collection and prevents illiquid firms from paying money they do not possess.

The legacy aggregate annual test harness retains its former fiscal estimator. Full monthly economic years use the material tax closure.

## Consequences

- Public revenue now has an explicit private payer and conserves cash.
- Revenue falls automatically when sales fall or firm liquidity is exhausted.
- Fiscal deficits and debt now reflect the gap between collected taxes and planned spending.
- The flat rate is deliberately coarse Stage 0 policy, not a complete tax system.
- Tax arrears, multiple tax bases, evasion, refunds, and configurable country rates remain future work.

## Validation

- The economic-year integration test proves assessed and paid tax equals 20 percent of realized sales in a liquid firm.
- The same test proves annual country revenue equals realized tax payment.
- A no-trade economic year continues to produce zero revenue and zero phantom output.
- A deterministic 50-year seed-47 run keeps production and trade active through 2074 while debt is generated from explicit collected revenue rather than an output-side revenue formula.
