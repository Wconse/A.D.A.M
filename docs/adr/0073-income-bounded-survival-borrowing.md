# ADR 0073: Income-bounded survival borrowing precedes state relief

- Status: Accepted foundation
- Date: 2026-07-23

Households now attempt a concrete private coping response before entering the monthly market. After income and scheduled debt service, each cohort calculates the current regional cost of its survival targets. If liquid wealth is insufficient, it may borrow the gap up to a total debt ceiling of twice aggregate annual income. Borrowed cash and debt enter the ordinary cohort ledgers, purchase ordinary market offers, and are serviced by the existing monthly debt cashflow.

The debt ceiling makes coping finite. It prevents unlimited credit from hiding persistent poverty and links borrowing capacity to the income that could plausibly service it. A replayed two-month integration scenario starts with monthly income of 50 against survival cost of 1,000: the cohort first borrows 950 and fully consumes, then services 7 of debt, borrows the remaining 257 of headroom, reaches the 1,200 ceiling, and can fund only 30% of survival demand. Health therefore deteriorates once private credit is exhausted.

The sequence is now `income/savings -> survival borrowing -> market -> health -> supply-aware public relief`. Government aid observes only the affordability gap left after household resources and bounded borrowing, so private coping and treasury response do not double-fund the same need.

The twice-income ceiling and ten-year straight-line debt service are explicit Stage 0 approximations. There are not yet lenders, interest, underwriting, collateral, default, bankruptcy, informal credit, household asset sales, or differentiated risk. Those should replace the aggregate credit rule after the complete coping and institutional response chain is readable in long runs.
