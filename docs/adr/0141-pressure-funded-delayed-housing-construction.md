# ADR 0141: Pressure-funded delayed public housing construction

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Housing scarcity can block otherwise useful migration, but fixed dwelling capacity would make successful regions permanently closed. Capacity must respond to observed pressure without appearing instantly or for free.

## Decision

At annual closure every region without an active project measures occupied households against dwelling capacity. Pressure of at least 9,000 basis points may start one public project. The project adds ten percent of existing capacity, rounded up to at least one dwelling, and costs twelve baseline monthly housing payments per dwelling.

The destination country must fund the complete cost from current treasury cash. Funding is debited immediately and retained as committed construction value. Physical dwellings appear only after two later annual construction advances. On completion the committed value becomes persistent public housing capital. Both active projects and completed capital are serialized and fingerprinted.

Projects are reviewed in canonical region order, so regions in the same country compete deterministically for scarce treasury. Typed start and completion events record pressure, cost, delay, delivered dwellings, and resulting capacity.

## Consequences

Housing pressure now creates a slow fiscal adaptation loop: employment and services attract households, occupancy raises relocation costs and can block migration, fees strengthen the treasury, and sufficiently funded states can expand capacity after a real delay. Weak treasuries remain constrained.

This is public construction without explicit contractors, materials, land, maintenance, rent, or planning politics. Committed cost becomes a real public capital stock rather than disappearing, but it is not yet paid through firm payroll and procurement. A later gate should create construction recipes and supplier payments before private development is introduced.
