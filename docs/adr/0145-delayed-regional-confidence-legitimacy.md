# ADR 0145: Delayed regional confidence affects legitimacy

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Material pressure already had an immediate national political effect. Persistent regional satisfaction needed a separate role or it would remain decorative, but applying the just-computed satisfaction in the same closure would make policy consequences instantaneous and double-count current pressure.

## Decision

Before each annual fiscal closure, country-level regional confidence is calculated from the prior authoritative regional satisfaction stocks, weighted by regional population. Neutral confidence is 5,000 basis points. Confidence contributes a bounded legitimacy signal from -200 to +200 basis points per year.

The current closure then records fiscal incidence and updates regional interests and satisfaction for use in the following year. This one-year ordering creates political memory: a government inherits the confidence produced by earlier outcomes rather than receiving an immediate reward for spending announced in the same closure. The signal remains separate from immediate material pressure, growth, fiscal balance, random political variation, and sovereign restructuring.

Typed evidence reports population-weighted confidence and its exact legitimacy effect. The chronicle names the strongest regional concern and explains the national confidence consequence.

## Consequences

Governments can recover after sustained improvements and lose support after accumulated unequal outcomes. Large regions influence confidence in proportion to population, while small administrative units cannot dominate by count. The model still lacks representation, organized constituencies, bargaining, elections, and policy selection based on regional interests; those are later gates built on this memory layer.
