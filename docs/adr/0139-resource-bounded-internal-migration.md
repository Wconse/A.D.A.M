# ADR 0139: Resource-bounded internal household migration

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Persistent unemployment, vacancies, public services, and household balance sheets existed independently. Without mobility, a region could retain unemployed households while another carried durable vacancies, and service investment could not influence settlement. Moving an entire aggregate cohort would be discontinuous and would duplicate or destroy its financial stocks.

## Decision

Annual closure may move at most one household partition from each source region. The move requires six months of excess local labor supply, six months of excess destination vacancies, an adult or mature unemployed cohort with no active contract or training, and enough liquid wealth to cover one migrant household's proportional monthly income as a relocation reserve. Destinations are domestic, ranked by persistent vacancies and the difference in healthcare, infrastructure, and administrative capacity, and rejected when services are more than 1,000 basis points worse.

The selected cohort is split deterministically. People, households, annual income, liquid wealth, and debt are partitioned proportionally; health, experience, stress memory, deprivation, and skills are inherited. Regional population totals move by the same exact amount. No money, debt, people, employment, or housing are created, and migration does not guarantee a job: the destination labor market must still hire the household through ordinary matching.

## Consequences

Migration is a slow structural response rather than instant labor teleportation. Wealth matters as mobility capacity, public services can attract or repel households, and destination vacancy pressure can ease over later labor-market months. The one-household-per-source annual bound prevents aggregate cohorts from oscillating or emptying abruptly.

This first slice omits international migration, housing prices and capacity, travel payments, family networks, migration preferences, return migration, and occupation-specific destination scoring. Those belong in later gates once housing and cross-border institutions are explicit.
