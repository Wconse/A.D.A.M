# ADR 0144: Persistent regional interests and fiscal incidence

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Regional social pressure affected national legitimacy, but regions had no durable policy concern and annual policy did not identify who paid and who received concrete benefits. Rebuilding a full party or election model now would add institutions before the material constituencies existed.

## Decision

Every region keeps one persistent leading policy interest: employment, household security, public services, or stability. The candidate interest is derived from the corresponding authoritative social-pressure component. A new concern replaces an incumbent only when it is at least 750 basis points stronger, except that a materially pressured region may leave baseline stability immediately. This hysteresis prevents annual oscillation.

Each annual closure also attributes paid firm sales tax to the firm's region and the recurring public-service allocation to its receiving region. Their difference classifies the region as a net contributor, balanced, or net beneficiary. This is a deliberately narrow fiscal-incidence ledger: it includes only taxes and services that the simulation can currently locate physically.

Regional satisfaction starts at 5,000 basis points. Improvement in the incumbent concern raises it; deterioration lowers it. Net beneficiaries receive a small positive annual signal and net contributors a symmetric negative signal. Annual movement is capped at 250 basis points. Interest, satisfaction, persistence, and the latest fiscal outcome are authoritative and fingerprinted. Typed evidence and chronicle narration identify priorities, contributors, beneficiaries, and net transfers.

## Consequences

Policy now creates named regional winners and losers without inventing ideology or electoral rules. Interests persist long enough to support future organization and bargaining, while still reacting to material change. The fiscal ledger is not a complete tax-benefit account: relief, reserves, infrastructure investment, debt service, and indirect incidence remain outside it until they can be assigned without guesswork.
