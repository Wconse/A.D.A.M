# A.D.A.M Simulation Plan

## Purpose

Stage 0 must prove that a deterministic model of people, institutions, production, information, and conflict can create history worth reading. Depth is designed globally but implemented through testable causal slices.

## Four realities

The simulation keeps four related but non-identical layers:

1. **Material reality** — people, health, goods, assets, production, inventories, transport, money, violence.
2. **Institutional reality** — laws, offices, ownership, procedures, jurisdictions, chains of command, veto points.
3. **Social reality** — trust, obligations, organizations, access, dependency, loyalty, fear, coalition membership.
4. **Information reality** — observations, beliefs, uncertainty, media frames, rumors, propaganda, public narratives.

An event can be materially true, unknown to an actor, misreported by media, disbelieved by an audience, and still change behavior. These states must never be collapsed into one global truth-visible-to-all value.

## Population and households

Population is represented by adaptive regional cohorts rather than one record per citizen. A cohort is differentiated when its behavior matters and merged when differences are immaterial.

Canonical dimensions:

- region and settlement type;
- age band and household composition;
- health and mortality risk;
- education, qualifications, and occupation;
- employment status and workplace sector;
- income sources, debt, liquid savings, and owned assets;
- housing tenure and housing quality;
- cultural, religious, linguistic, and political identities where relevant;
- migration status and social-network anchors.

Demography includes births, deaths, aging, household formation, education pipelines, internal migration, immigration, and emigration. Every process is linked to material conditions and institutions.

## Household demand

Demand originates from population cohorts and actual budgets:

```text
wages + transfers + property income - taxes - debt service = spendable resources
```

Needs are hierarchical but substitutable:

- survival: calories, water, shelter, heat, essential medicine;
- participation: transport, communication, clothing, childcare;
- development: education, improved health, durable goods;
- discretionary: leisure, travel, prestige, savings.

A need can be served by several goods with different price, quality, time cost, infrastructure requirements, habits, and cultural fit. Shortages create substitution, unmet need, deteriorating health, lost labor time, black markets, migration pressure, and political response.

## Production

Production units combine:

- workers by qualification;
- land and location;
- buildings and equipment;
- energy;
- intermediate inputs;
- technology and organizational knowledge;
- management capacity;
- credit and working capital;
- logistics access.

Firms plan from expected demand and observed prices, not future truth. They can misforecast, overinvest, hold inventory, ration, lay off workers, fail, merge, lobby, or survive through political protection.

## Markets and logistics

Markets are spatial and inventory-based. Orders are matched over regional networks with transport cost, capacity, delay, tariffs, sanctions, risk, and spoilage. Prices respond to fill rate, stock-to-sales ratios, cost, market power, expectations, and credit conditions rather than instant perfect equilibrium.

Initial goods are deliberately limited to a causal minimum: staple food, energy, housing services, transport, basic manufactures, healthcare, education, and capital goods. New goods are added only when they create qualitatively new behavior.

## Labor and human capital

Jobs require skill bundles, location, schedule, and working conditions. Workers evaluate expected wage, commute, housing, security, status, and migration cost. Education takes time and capacity; skill shortages cannot be solved instantly. Unemployment, underemployment, labor bargaining, automation, and occupational mobility feed back into household demand and politics.

## Government and finance

Government has real revenue, expenditure, debt, assets, procurement, administrative capacity, and legal constraints. Passing policy does not guarantee implementation. Banks and capital markets allocate credit under incomplete information; insolvency, liquidity crises, collateral, risk concentration, and political guarantees matter.

## Power and influence

There is no universal stored influence score. Outcomes are resolved for a specific action from:

- legal authority and veto points;
- organizational control;
- material resources;
- social relationships and obligations;
- information access;
- audience-specific public legitimacy;
- coalition support;
- opposition capacity;
- actor beliefs and uncertainty.

Existing scalar influence edges are a temporary deterministic scaffold and must be replaced by typed relationship dimensions and context-specific resolution before political gameplay is accepted.

## Multi-rate time

Systems run only as often as their causal timescale requires:

- orders, inventories, and prices: daily or weekly batches;
- wages, production plans, and household budgets: monthly;
- migration and education transitions: quarterly or annual cohorts;
- births, deaths, fiscal closure, and structural statistics: annual;
- crises, battles, elections, and negotiations: event-driven.

Aggregation may change frequency, never conserved quantities or causal direction.

## Depth rule

A proposed subsystem is accepted when it adds at least one of:

- a new meaningful decision;
- a new causal feedback loop;
- a new observable historical pattern;
- a new failure mode that actors can anticipate or misunderstand;
- a new source of conflict between actors or groups.

It is rejected or postponed when it only adds labels, coefficients, repetitive micro-management, or detail with no downstream consequence.

## Implementation discipline

Each slice must define invariants, input data, system order, observable outputs, determinism tests, causal tests, scale probes, and removal criteria for temporary approximations. History-changing changes bump the simulation version. Schema-breaking content changes bump the world schema version.
