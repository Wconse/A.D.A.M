# Information, Reputation, and Propaganda

## Chosen model

Public reputation uses a hybrid of:

- **audience-specific trait beliefs**; and
- **event memory plus competing narratives**.

Reputation is not popularity and media spending never directly purchases support.

## Separate state

For each relevant audience and actor, the model distinguishes:

- awareness: whether the audience knows the actor;
- attention: whether the actor is currently salient;
- trait beliefs: competence, integrity, strength, empathy, consistency, ideological alignment;
- issue beliefs: perceived positions and responsibility for outcomes;
- confidence: how certain the audience is;
- affect: emotional attachment or hostility;
- willingness to support publicly;
- private belief;
- expected behavior under social or coercive pressure.

Public compliance, sincere belief, and private doubt are separate. A dictatorship can display near-unanimous public support while private beliefs remain fragmented and poorly measured.

## Claims and narratives

Information circulates as claims, not direct stat changes. A claim contains:

- subject and proposition;
- claimed cause and responsibility;
- supporting evidence or fabricated evidence;
- source and transmission chain;
- emotional frame;
- targeted audience;
- novelty and repetition;
- verifiability;
- relation to known material events.

Narratives connect claims into causal explanations such as decline, betrayal, restoration, conspiracy, competence, victimhood, or external threat.

## Why absurd propaganda can work

A claim does not need to be objectively plausible to spread. Reception depends on:

- monopoly or concentration of information sources;
- repetition and familiarity;
- trust in the source;
- distrust of all alternatives;
- identity-protective reasoning;
- fear and social conformity;
- prior compatible beliefs;
- emotional usefulness;
- low ability to verify;
- elite coordination and apparent social consensus;
- censorship, punishment, or career incentives;
- crisis uncertainty;
- humor, irony, and meme mutation.

Absurd propaganda may produce different outcomes:

- sincere belief;
- partial belief;
- belief that “something must be wrong” without accepting the literal claim;
- public repetition without private belief;
- demoralization and withdrawal from politics;
- loyalty signaling;
- backlash and loss of source credibility.

The system must support all of these. Propaganda success is not simply persuasion; confusing the information environment or making truth unknowable can itself be an objective.

## Media campaign mechanics

A campaign chooses:

- audience;
- message and frame;
- channels;
- frequency;
- messengers;
- evidence strategy;
- emotional tone;
- timing;
- budget and organizational effort.

Spending buys production, reach, targeting, repetition, distribution, and sometimes suppression of alternatives. Belief updates arise afterward from audience priors, source credibility, personal experience, counterclaims, and social transmission.

## Source credibility

Credibility is domain-specific and path-dependent. A source can be trusted on economics but distrusted on war. Correct predictions, visible corrections, exposed lies, censorship, ownership, and coordination with other sources alter credibility. Repeated lying can still work under monopoly or coercion, but it changes the mechanism from trust to conformity, confusion, or lack of alternatives.

## Rumors and mutation

Claims can mutate during social transmission:

- details simplify;
- causal responsibility shifts;
- uncertainty disappears;
- emotionally useful elements survive;
- separate stories merge;
- satire is reinterpreted literally;
- official denial becomes evidence for a conspiracy.

Mutation is deterministic for a seed and transmission context, with isolated random streams.

## Observation and polling

No actor sees true public opinion. Available instruments include polls, focus groups, elections, sales and mobility data, social-media signals, informants, security reports, and personal networks. Each has sampling error, coverage bias, manipulation risk, delay, and an institutional incentive to please the recipient.

## Minimal implementation slice

1. Material event produces factual claims.
2. Two media sources frame the event differently.
3. Several audience cohorts receive claims through different channels.
4. Awareness, trait beliefs, confidence, and source credibility update.
5. Private belief and publicly expressed belief can diverge.
6. A later election, protest, purchase, or compliance decision uses those beliefs.
7. Chronicle records both the event and the narratives that shaped its interpretation.
